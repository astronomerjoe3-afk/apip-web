# School Pilot Materials

## Pilot Name

Cognispark Physics Misconception Repair Pilot

## Pilot Promise

Cognispark helps students understand physics concepts before they calculate, while giving teachers clearer insight into the misconceptions behind wrong answers.

## Target Pilot

Best first pilot:

- one physics teacher
- one to three classes
- 30 to 120 students
- 4 weeks
- topic: M1 Motion and Kinematics

## Pilot Goals

- improve student confidence with motion graphs
- reduce repeated graph-reading misconceptions
- give teachers a faster way to identify class weaknesses
- generate credible learning-gain and usability evidence

## Pilot Timeline

| Week | Activity | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Setup | teacher onboarding, class roster, baseline assessment |
| 1 | Public mission and M1.1 | graph-as-record misconception baseline |
| 2 | M1.2 and M1.3 | distance, displacement, speed, velocity, slope meaning |
| 3 | M1.4 and M1.5 | velocity-time area and acceleration meaning |
| 4 | Capstone and review | post assessment, teacher report, student survey |

## Teacher One-Pager

### What Cognispark Does

Cognispark is an interactive physics learning platform that turns lessons into guided missions. Students learn through visual setup, live reasoning tools, checkpoint feedback, and targeted correction.

### What Makes It Different

Most platforms show whether an answer is wrong. Cognispark tries to identify why it is wrong. The teacher sees the misconception pattern, not only the score.

### What The Pilot Measures

- graph interpretation improvement
- repeated misconception reduction
- mission completion
- student confidence
- teacher time saved in diagnosis

### Teacher Commitment

- 20 minutes for setup
- one assigned Cognispark mission per week
- 10 minutes per week reviewing class dashboard
- short feedback form at end of pilot

## Baseline And Post Assessment

Focus: motion graph interpretation.

Question types:

- identify stopped segment
- compare greatest speed by gradient
- distinguish graph shape from path
- interpret negative velocity
- use area under velocity-time graph
- explain acceleration from velocity change

Measure:

- first-attempt accuracy
- misconception selected
- explanation quality
- post-assessment gain

## Pilot Success Criteria

Minimum success:

- 70 percent of active students complete at least two missions
- average post-assessment score improves by 15 percent or more
- at least 50 percent of students who missed a misconception item improve on delayed review
- teacher reports dashboard was useful for identifying weak concepts

Strong success:

- 80 percent mission completion
- 25 percent or higher post-assessment gain
- teacher reports meaningful reduction in diagnosis or marking time
- school agrees to extend pilot to another class or topic
