# Competitor Matrix

## Strategic Summary

Cognispark should not copy the biggest platforms. It should take a narrower but sharper position:

> the interactive physics platform that diagnoses misconceptions and repairs conceptual reasoning through guided missions.

The global leaders have advantages in content scale, brand, institutional adoption, research backing, and free distribution. Cognispark can compete by being more focused, more diagnostic, and more coherent as a physics reasoning coach.

## Benchmark Table

| Platform | Strength | Weakness Cognispark Can Exploit | What Cognispark Should Learn |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Khan Academy | Free, trusted, broad physics courses, videos, practice, mastery | Physics interactivity is not always deep or simulation-native | Standards alignment, learner dashboards, teacher-friendly course structure |
| PhET | World-class research-based interactive simulations | Simulations are often exploratory, not always full guided curriculum | Make invisible physics visible, use intuitive controls, measure exploration |
| Brilliant | Premium interactive problem-solving and polished learning design | Less school-specific, less physics-only depth than a specialist could offer | Short interactive lessons, beautiful pacing, immediate feedback |
| Isaac Science | Strong UK school adoption, massive question attempts, teacher workflows, structured hints | More problem-practice oriented than visual mission platform | Teacher dashboards, low-stakes persistence, data-informed hints |
| Pearson Mastering Physics | Institution-grade homework, analytics, textbook ecosystem, AI study tools | Can feel corporate, expensive, and less delightful for independent learners | Assignment bank, analytics, LMS readiness, remediation |
| MIT OpenCourseWare | University credibility and deep open materials | Not beginner-friendly as an adaptive learning product | Academic seriousness, rich source material, credibility |
| OpenStax | Free textbook depth, accepted scope and sequence, practice | Mostly textbook-first, not interactive-first | Scope coverage, outcomes, instructor resources |
| Physiworld and similar indie platforms | Visual, gamified physics appeal | Often weaker on evidence, curriculum rigor, or school workflows | Public demos, gamified motivation, low-friction entry |

## Strategic Wedge

Cognispark should lead with:

- "Physics misconceptions repaired, not hidden."
- "Every mission teaches the idea before the formula."
- "Teachers see what students misunderstand, not just who got it wrong."

Avoid leading with:

- "We have more physics content than everyone."
- "We are a complete replacement for all physics platforms immediately."
- "AI tutor" without evidence, guardrails, and domain-specific behavior.

## Best Target Segment

Primary beachhead:

- high-school physics students struggling with graphs, forces, and energy
- teachers who want better conceptual diagnosis without extra marking
- schools that need visual, guided physics support but do not want a full LMS migration

Secondary segment:

- self-study students preparing for GCSE, IGCSE, AP Physics 1, or first mechanics courses

## Proof Required To Enter The Top Tier

Cognispark needs visible proof in five categories:

- learning proof: pre/post gains, misconception repair rates, retention after spaced review
- product proof: polished public mission, screenshots, demos, sample dashboards
- teacher proof: pilot testimonials, assignment workflows, class progress reports
- trust proof: domain support, privacy, terms, accessibility, deletion, security posture
- curriculum proof: standards alignment and full module samples
